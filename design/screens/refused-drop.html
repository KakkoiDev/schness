Here only a knight may give check on a drop, so the same five squares are illegal for a rook — a4, c4, d4, b3 and b2 are not targets. But refusedDrops finds refusals by re-running with checking drops enabled, which does not lift this restriction, so they are drawn as nothing at all: no target dot, no stripe, no sentence. These eighteen need a new comparison, not new wording. The copy below is a proposal.

Proposed copy for the eighteen

One sentence each, in the same shape as the Classic line: what is not allowed, then what happened to your piece. Japanese is the constraint on length, not English.

Which piece may check
Only a knight may give check on a drop. Your piece is still in reserve.
Where a check may land
A checking drop must land further from the king. Your piece is still in reserve.
When checks unlock
Checking drops unlock after both players deploy a piece. Your piece is still in reserve.
How often
You have already made your checking drop this game. Your piece is still in reserve.
Setup phase second gap
Setup drops may not give check. Your piece is still in reserve.

The setup line is the one nobody has yet: all four full-setup presets require quiet drops during setup, but the guard exits whenever phase is not play, so a refused setup drop is never explained — in the phase a new player meets first.
