The capture-bank tray

Classic’s tray holds three fixed slots because Classic always returns a piece to the player who owned it. A capture bank holds between three and six pieces you took from your opponent, and when it is full the next capture goes back to them instead. None of that is currently visible: the tray is the Classic tray with a ×N badge.

Classic — the baseline

Your reserve

Three slots, always three. Capacity is implicit and never changes, so no number is needed. An empty slot is a piece on the board, and it can come back.

Bank, partly full

Your bank3 / 6

Slots are drawn to the capacity, not to the piece types. Three of six held: you can read both the count and the ceiling without a number — and the number is there anyway, because colour and position are never the only signal.

Bank, fullchanges the rules

Your bank4 / 4

Every slot taken. This state changes what a capture does, so it is drawn, not merely counted: the next piece you take will go back to its owner rather than to you. It is also where a capacity-3 bank starts: both sides begin holding a rook, bishop and knight, so every capture returns to its owner until you deploy. A full bank at capacity 6 cannot overflow — it holds all six non-kings, so there is nothing left to take.

Duplicates

Your bank4 / 6

Two rooks occupy two slots — yours and the one you took. Each type exists twice in the whole game, so two is the most a tray can hold. The shipped ×N badge compressed exactly the thing you need to read, so this drops the badge: a duplicate is another slot, because that is what it costs you.

Empty

Your bank0 / 4

Capacity still legible with nothing held. Four dashed slots say “four, when you take them” — a bank you have not filled yet, not a bank that cannot fill.

Your reserve1 left of 3

The one state that must not look like “empty”. Here two pieces have left the game and will not return; one knight remains. A dashed slot can refill, a struck slot cannot, and today both would be drawn the same dashed box.

The moment of overflow

The only place the rule is legible is when it fires. Capture bank 4, bank full, and you take Black’s bishop. The captured piece animates to the tray that actually receives it — which is the opponent’s, once yours is full.

Black reserve1

Your bank is full — the bishop went back to Black.

This keeps the existing invariant honest: the taken piece flies to the tray that gets it. Classic, the owner’s. Capture bank with room, yours. Capture bank when full, the owner’s again. Permanent loss, nowhere — it fades on the square.

What the engine does not track

Verified by running applyAction, not by reading it. Capacity counts bank entries only; a deployed piece frees its slot. There are six non-kings in the game, two of each type, and both banks start holding one of each.

A bank holds types, not pieces
banks.white is ["rook","rook","knight"]. There is no owner and no origin on a bank entry.
A dropped piece is yours
occupantOf(WHITE, ROOK) → {owner:"white", piece:"rook"}. A rook taken from Black returns to the board white.
So “opponent-origin pieces” do not exist
The brief asks the tray to show a piece’s colour and owner after a transfer, and to show a drop from a bank holding opponent-origin pieces. Neither is in the data. Drawing a captured piece in Black’s colour would be a lie about what happens when you drop it.
Decided: animation only 8 Nov
The user chose to keep bank entries as plain piece types. The transfer animation carries “where it went” and the tray stays in your colour, because under capture-bank rules the piece really is yours. No origin field: it would add engine state that replays and research archives hash, for a mark with no effect on play.

Engineering prerequisites

  • bankCapacity reaches the renderer. renderReserve takes the pieces and nothing else; bankCapacity appears nowhere in board-ui.js or watch-ui.js. Capacity has to be passed before any of this can be drawn.
  • A full bank is a state, not a count. The renderer needs to know it is at capacity so the tray can say what the next capture will do.
  • Permanent loss needs its own slot state. Distinguishable from empty, and derived from the ruleset rather than from the number of pieces held.
  • The transfer animation needs a destination. It already flies to victim.owner; under capture-bank it must fly to the recipient the engine chose, which is the capturer when banks[player].length < bankCapacity and the victim otherwise.

Proposed. Trays are drawn with the shipped .bank, .bank-piece and .bank-slot classes; the count, the full state and the struck slot are new and marked PROPOSED in design.css.
