# Archive-only test of duplicate reserve material

Checkpoint: PR #123 b4dd4af45b569943d1a6c479b3f20db4a2945818. Schness adds material for every copy of a reserve piece, while the legal generator offers one drop action per type and destination. A possible valuation discrepancy is that identical extra copies have less immediate utility. This is distinct from the closed global ridge fit and reserve king-pressure experiments. Do not fit any coefficient.

Replay all 16 frozen games and join all 964 timed decisions by game, ply, and action. For every disagreement, apply each legal action separately to the same full-history position. A duplicate is each reserve copy past the first of its type. Report both child duplicate balances (mover duplicates minus opponent duplicates), before/after banks, move classes, captured/deployed piece types, and terminal status. Exclude terminal children, mixed move classes, and pairs with unequal material after the move from the primary comparison; report every exclusion and raw row. Use the unchanged Schness material subtotal: board rook/bishop/knight 520/340/320, reserve 500/320/300, kings ignored as equal in legal play. Use mover minus opponent in both children. This avoids mistaking a material trade or a drop-versus-capture preference for the proposed effect.

Primary measure: among eligible pairs with unequal duplicate balances, does Fairy choose the child with fewer net duplicates than the timed Schness choice? Require at least 24 informative pairs spanning at least eight games and a two-to-one count favoring fewer duplicates. Keep ties separate; report by move class and game without changing the gate. If it fails, stop this archival hypothesis without changing inclusion rules or adding data. If it passes, it permits a separate fresh, matched experiment plan only. A duplicate can still have tactical value; this observational comparison cannot identify causality or strength. No engine search, training, or benchmark change.
